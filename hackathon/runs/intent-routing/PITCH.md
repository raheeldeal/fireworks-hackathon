# PITCH.md: banking intent routing (3 minutes)

1. **Problem and who pays (20s):** "Every message to a digital bank's support inbox is sorted into one of 77 intents before a human sees it: card not arrived, refund missing, PIN blocked. Even the best open model gets 1 in 8 wrong. At 50,000 messages a day that is 180,000 misroutes a month, each one a human re-triage and a slower answer. The support operations lead pays for both."
2. **Task and eval (20s):** "We used Banking77, real customer messages with 77 labels. We locked a stratified test set, two messages per intent, 154 rows, hashed, and score exact label match automatically. Anything outside the 77 labels counts as invalid. We ran every number with temperature zero and re-ran the key configs on four row orders to measure noise: ±2.6 points."
3. **Baseline (20s):** "Off-the-shelf small model, glm-5p3-flash: 85% at 26 cents per thousand messages. Frontier open model, kimi-k3: 88% at $5.60 per thousand, 22 times the price, and 50% slower."
4. **What we changed (40s):** [diagram] "The small model answers every message and reports a confidence. Below a threshold, or if it emits an invalid label, we escalate that one message to the big model. We swept seven thresholds against three escalation targets, offline and exactly, from the per-row results. We also tried the usual prompt tricks: a rubric built from the misses, five-shot, enum-constrained JSON, thinking on and off. Most of those made the small model *worse*; the JSON schema did kill all invented labels; and turning thinking off made Nemotron 7x faster at no accuracy cost."
5. **Result (40s):** [table] "Routing at confidence 0.70 escalates 27% of traffic and lands exactly on the frontier's 88%, at $1.74 per thousand instead of $5.60, with p50 latency of 0.67 seconds instead of 1.8. If you accept 84%, escalate to gpt-oss-120b instead: 30 cents per thousand, 19 times cheaper than the frontier. And the thing that did not work: Nemotron's confidence is flat, 95% on two-thirds of messages, so there is nothing to route on."
6. **Business value (20s):** "At 1.5 million messages a month, frontier accuracy for $2,600 instead of $8,400, no extra misroutes, and the customer gets an answer a second faster. The honest number: model cost is small next to re-triage labour, so the accuracy points are what matter, and routing gives you the frontier's points at a third of the price."
7. **How far we pushed it (20s):** [frontier chart] "Here is the whole frontier: six models, five prompts, three routing curves. The curve beats every single-model point. What it also shows: on this task the frontier does not struggle much. The remaining 12% is mostly label ambiguity in the dataset. So the next step is not a bigger model; it is a cleaner taxonomy, and a fine-tune that learns the house conventions, which is ready to run on Fireworks' free Muse Glimmer SFT (blocked tonight by the one-run-per-model training slot)."
